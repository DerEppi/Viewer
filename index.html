3D velocity field of the Taurus molecular cloud complex

Interactive viewer for a velocity field reconstructed from Gaia DR3 astrometry and radial velocities using Bayesian information field theory (NIFTy). Runs entirely in the browser — no installation, nothing is uploaded.

Features

  • Reference frames — heliocentric, relative to the Taurus bulk motion, the LSR, or a custom vector.
  • Colour by anything — velocity components, divergence, vorticity, posterior uncertainty, stellar age, mass, membership group.
  • Adjustable mask — move the density threshold to see where the field is constrained by data and where it extrapolates.
  • Streamlines and 2D plots — animated tracers, histograms, scatter plots, colour–magnitude diagrams, power spectra.
  • Export — screenshots and videos.

Other runs

The same viewer with a different reconstruction embedded, as light versions. Press I inside any of them for its exact parameters and convergence record.

test_with_sigma rewritten pipeline Same data and grid as the default run, but the stars are allowed a motion of their own on top of the field: one common dispersion is inferred alongside the field and added to every measurement uncertainty, so a deviating star no longer has to be absorbed by bending the field towards it. open →
test_no_sigma_seed rewritten pipeline A repetition of the default run with a different random seed. The seed controls every random number the inference uses: the field it starts from, and the samples it draws to trace the posterior. A different seed sends the run along a different path, which separates the features the data demand from those the algorithm was free to invent. open →
test_no_sigma_seed2 rewritten pipeline A second repetition with yet another seed. Here the prior on the power spectrum is also pushed towards smoother fields, which suppresses structure on the smallest scales. open →
parameters_old original pipeline One of the first runs, with the early priors, which left the field free to vary strongly and on small scales. The noise estimation is active, radial velocities are restricted to −30…30 km/s and the RUWE cut comes from a position-dependent Gaia query, leaving 89 stars with a radial velocity. It carries the same code error as ruwe_test, but shows the usual behaviour of the noise estimation: a median η of 5 inflates the radial-velocity errors by more than a factor of two, against 1.95 in ruwe_test. open →
ruwe_test original pipeline The exception among the runs with an active noise estimation: instead of discarding the radial velocities it only inflates their errors by about 40 per cent (median η of 1.95), so they keep constraining the field and the split along U that the other such runs develop stays away. The RUWE threshold is fixed at 1.4 here rather than taken from the position-dependent query, and a code error let 112 stars into the fit where the cuts should have kept 114. open →
test_new_param original pipeline A later run of the original pipeline, close to the setup the rewritten pipeline still uses: no noise estimation, and a much tighter prior on how strongly the field may vary. The RUWE cut is dropped in favour of a cut on the radial-velocity error, which keeps 156 stars with a radial velocity, and the field offsets are centred on the mean motion of Galli et al. (2019). open →
